The Leather Care Guide

Good leather gets better with age, as long as it is cleaned gently, conditioned sparingly and stored with a little thought.

In this guide

  1. Know your leather
  2. Everyday cleaning
  3. Conditioning
  4. Storage
  5. Scratches, stains and rain
  6. When to see a professional

Know your leather

LeatherCharacterCare note
Smooth calfFine, even surfaceShows scratches; condition lightly
PebbledGrained, forgivingHides wear; brush dust from the grain
PatentHigh gloss coatingWipe only; keep away from other colours
SuedeSoft napped finishBrush, never condition; avoid rain
EmbossedPressed textureClean with a soft brush in the pattern

Everyday cleaning

Wipe the outside with a soft, dry cloth after use. For marks, use a cloth very slightly dampened with water and dry it straight away. Always test any cleaner on a hidden area, such as under a flap, before using it on the whole bag.

Conditioning

Conditioner replaces oils that leather loses over time. Apply a small amount with a soft cloth in circular motions, leave it to absorb, then buff. For most bags, every three to six months is enough. The Leather Care Planner suggests a schedule for your leather and how often you carry it.

Storage

  • Stuff lightly with acid-free tissue to keep the shape.
  • Store in a breathable dust bag, never plastic.
  • Keep away from direct sun and radiators.
  • Wrap chains or metal hardware in tissue so they do not mark the leather.
  • Store small bags upright or flat, not squashed under heavier ones.

Scratches, stains and rain

Light scratches on smooth leather can often be softened by rubbing gently with a clean fingertip. Water: blot, do not rub, then let the bag dry naturally away from heat. Ink and oil stains are difficult; a professional leather cleaner is the safest choice. Colour transfer from dark clothes onto pale bags is common, so choose carefully what you carry a light bag against.

When to see a professional

Torn stitching, broken hardware, peeling edge paint and deep stains are all repairable by a good leather workshop. Repairing early stops small damage from spreading, and is usually far cheaper than replacing the bag.
