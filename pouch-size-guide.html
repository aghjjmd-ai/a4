The Pouch Size Guide

Small bags are unforgiving: a centimetre too narrow and your phone won’t close inside. Here is how to get the size right first time.

In this guide

  1. Measure the one thing that decides
  2. Size chart
  3. Depth matters more than you think
  4. Strap drop and how it sits
  5. Measuring a bag you already own
  6. Using the Size Finder

Measure the one thing that decides

In almost every small bag, the phone sets the minimum size. Measure yours in its case, then add at least two centimetres to the length and one to the width. For a zip pouch, add a little more: the zip tape and teeth take up room at both ends.

Size chart

SizeTypical interiorFits
Micro12 × 9 cmCards, lipstick, one key
Mini18 × 11 cmCompact phone, cards, lipstick
Small22 × 13 cmStandard phone, card holder, keys
Medium pouch26 × 16 cmLarge phone, small wallet, sunglasses
Large pouch30 × 20 cmEverything above plus a charger or notebook

Interior sizes vary between makers, so always check the listed measurements rather than the name.

Depth matters more than you think

A flat pouch with no gusset may hold a phone, but add keys and a lipstick and it bulges. A base or gusset of three to five centimetres gives a small bag real capacity while keeping a slim look. Structured shapes, such as a box clutch, hold less than their outside size suggests.

Strap drop and how it sits

  • Wrist loop: around 15 cm; the bag hangs just below the hand.
  • Shoulder: a drop of 20 to 25 cm sits under the arm.
  • Crossbody: 50 to 60 cm usually sits at the hip; taller people may want more.

A detachable or adjustable strap makes a single small bag work for many occasions.

Measuring a bag you already own

  1. Lay the bag flat and close it.
  2. Measure the width along the base, not the top.
  3. Measure height from the base to the opening.
  4. Measure depth at the widest part of the base.

Use these figures to compare with any new bag you are considering.
